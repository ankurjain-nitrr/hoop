# Principles

These principles apply to all work: writing a document, building a change, reviewing either, or
deciding between options. They come from the published principles of Google, Amazon, Anthropic and
Databricks, grouped where they say the same thing. Each one says what it asks of the work and what
a reviewer looks for.

## 1. Start from the customer

Begin with the people the work is for, and work back to the solution. Judge every choice by its
effect on them.
- In the work: name the user, their problem and what changes for them.
- In a review: ask who is affected and how, and flag work that serves the builder more than the user.
- Sources: Amazon's Customer Obsession, Google's "Focus on the user and all else will follow",
  Databricks' "We are customer obsessed", Anthropic's "Be good to our users".

## 2. Own the outcome, for the long term

Act for the whole product and its future, not only your part or this release. Never say "that's
not my job".
- In the work: consider what this change does to the rest of the system and to later work.
- In a review: ask what the change costs or rules out later, and who else it affects.
- Sources: Amazon's Ownership and Think Big, Databricks' "We put the company first", Anthropic's
  "Act for the global good".

## 3. Do the simple thing that works

Find the simplest solution that solves the real problem, then improve it. Do one thing really well
before adding the next.
- In the work: remove parts that don't earn their place, and reason from first principles.
- In a review: ask whether a simpler shape, an existing part or a smaller first step would do.
- Sources: Anthropic's "Do the simple thing that works" and Amazon's Invent and Simplify. Also
  Google's "It's best to do one thing really, really well" and Databricks' first principles thinking.

## 4. Bias for action

Speed matters, and most decisions can be changed later. Decide, act and learn.
- In the work: take the reversible decision now, and slow down only for decisions that are hard to
  undo.
- In a review: don't hold up work for questions that can be answered later. Name the one-way
  decisions, and ask for care only there.
- Sources: Amazon's Bias for Action, Databricks' "We bias for action", Google's "Fast is better
  than slow".

## 5. A high bar, reached in steps

Keep the standard high, and reach it through iteration rather than one perfect attempt.
- In the work: ship a version that is good and complete for its scope, then raise it.
- In a review: hold the bar on what matters to the user and on correctness. Leave polish for later
  versions, and mark it as non-blocking.
- Sources: Amazon's Insist on the Highest Standards, Google's "Great just isn't good enough",
  Databricks' "We raise the bar", Anthropic's "start simple and iterate".

## 6. Seek the truth

Decide from data and evidence, dig into the details, and change your view when the facts change.
- In the work: check claims against the source, measure what you can, and state what is assumed.
- In a review: ask for the evidence behind a claim, and read the source when it matters.
- Sources: Databricks' "We are truth seeking", Amazon's Dive Deep, Are Right A Lot and Learn and Be
  Curious, Google's "There's always more information out there".

## 7. Earn trust

Be honest, direct and transparent, especially about problems and limits. Be helpful, honest and
harmless.
- In the work: say plainly what is done, what isn't and what is uncertain.
- In a review: be candid and kind, and assume good intent.
- Sources: Amazon's Earn Trust, Anthropic's "Be helpful, honest, and harmless".

## 8. Have backbone, then commit

Disagree openly and with reasons while a question is open. Once the owner decides, commit fully.
- In the work: when you make a call, record why, so others can follow and challenge it.
- In a review: argue your view once with evidence, accept the owner's call, and don't reopen it
  without new facts.
- Sources: Amazon's Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit, Databricks' "debate fast, plan fast, drive
  alignment".

## 9. Weigh harm and benefit

Take both the risks and the benefits seriously. Success and scale bring a duty to avoid harm.
- In the work: name who could be hurt, how, and what prevents it.
- In a review: check privacy, security and safety at the level the work affects them.
- Sources: Anthropic's "Hold light and shade", Amazon's Success and Scale Bring Broad
  Responsibility, Google's "You can make money without doing evil".

## 10. Do more with less

Constraints breed ingenuity. Spend effort, time and money where they matter most.
- In the work: prefer what exists over new parts, and small changes over large ones.
- In a review: question cost that doesn't serve the user, in effort, time or complexity.
- Sources: Amazon's Frugality, Anthropic's "Do the simple thing that works".

## 11. Enable, don't block

Help the work move ahead, and make the people around you better.
- In the work: leave things easier for the next person, through clear docs, tests and names.
- In a review: pair every objection with a way forward. Write "this would work if" rather than
  "don't do this".
- Sources: Amazon's Hire and Develop the Best and Deliver Results, Databricks' "We raise the bar".
